Research questions

Coordination and markets

A currency is useful if other people will accept it. Experimental markets let us watch that expectation form and see what happens when inflation changes.

Read the currency study

Preferences and production decisions

Which pasture management practices would Uruguayan cattle producers adopt, and at what cost? Choice experiments reveal how producers weigh the alternatives.

Read the livestock study

Beliefs and public policy

People who receive the same information can disagree about what to do. We study how political loyalties relate to expectations and support for public policies.

Selected publications

Papers by our researchers and their coauthors.

2024Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization

On the emergence of international currencies: An experimental approach

Cardozo, Rosokha & Zhang

When will a seller accept another country’s currency? In the laboratory, buyers and sellers often settled on using both currencies. Higher foreign inflation made sellers less willing to take foreign money.

2026Journal of Agricultural Economics

Preferences for Sustainable Production Practices in Extensive Livestock Systems in Uruguay

Guerrero Escobar, Kuhfuss, Rivero Wildemauwe, Carriquiry, Rosas, Correa Garcia & Velazco de Los Reyes

Which changes would cattle producers adopt on their farms? A choice experiment in Uruguay found that producers differed in the compensation they would require to change grazing and land management practices.

2021Humanities and Social Sciences Communications

The interplay between partisanship, forecasted COVID-19 deaths, and support for preventive policies

Freira, Sartorio, Boruchowicz, López Bóo & Navajas

How do political loyalties enter a public health decision? The researchers examined forecasts of pandemic deaths and support for preventive measures in Argentina, Uruguay, Brazil, and the United States.
